# If this were a real product: what I'd do next, and why

Source: `docs/usage_report.html` — 30 simulated users x 45 days, same seed across
runs, generated by `scripts/simulate.py`. Numbers below are the last 15 simulated
days. **Caveat up front:** the data is synthetic (templated language, hand-built
distractors, one seed, ~900 eval queries per run, roughly +/-3 points of noise),
so treat it as evidence for *direction*, not magnitude. Item 3 exists because of
that.

## What the data says

| Run | Mem/user | Hit@5 | Current fact #1 | Stale outranks current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current defaults (0.6/0.2/0.2, decay on) | 41 | 37% | 19% | 1% |
| Decay job off | 110 | 44% | 22% | 1% |
| Recency weight 0.4 | 41 | 22% | 10% | 1% |
| Recency weight 0 | 42 | **80%** | 46% | **23%** |

1. **Retrieval quality is the problem, not scale.** Hit@5 falls from 100% (day 1)
   to ~40% as the store grows, while search p95 stays ~16ms and the decay job
   takes 0.11s per simulated day for all 30 users. Latency and storage are not
   where the product hurts.
2. **A single global recency term forces a bad trade.** Recency is what stops
   superseded facts winning (1% vs 23%), but it also buries old durable facts
   under fresh chatter (37% vs 80% hit rate). No weight setting escapes this:
   the two rows are two points on one frontier. Recency treats "I live in
   Lisbon" and "meeting moved to Tuesday" as the same kind of thing.
3. **Decay bounds growth as designed** (41 vs 110 memories/user and rising) **but
   cost ~7 points of hit rate** (37% vs 44%). My hypothesis, *not tested*: decay
   is type-blind and erodes durable facts that simply aren't queried often.

## Priorities

1. **Stop scoring facts and episodes the same way.** Add a memory kind
   (fact vs episode) and apply recency only to episodes; for facts, handle
   change with supersession (a newer, highly similar memory demotes the older
   one) instead of age. Why first: it targets the ~40-point loss, and the
   ablation shows weight tuning alone can't. Success test, using the simulator
   we already have: hit@5 >= 80% *and* stale-outranks < 5% in the same run.
2. **Make decay kind-aware.** Protect facts, expire episodes. Small change
   once (1) exists; test is that decay-on matches decay-off hit rate at ~40%
   of the storage.
3. **Replace the synthetic eval with real signal.** Log each query, the
   returned ids/scores, and (optionally) a "was this useful" callback from the
   agent. Without this, everything above is tuned to my own simulator. Needed
   before trusting any of the numbers, so it ships alongside (1).

## Deliberately not now

- **More scaling work / a dedicated vector DB:** no evidence of need at
  ~100 memories per user and ~16ms search; Phase 4 already covers the load
  story. Revisit with real per-user volumes.
- **Auth (Phase 6)** is required before real users, but doesn't move retrieval
  quality, so it doesn't compete with the items above for "what improves the
  product."
- **Weight tuning** as a standalone project: the frontier above says it's
  moving along a curve, not improving it.

## Known gaps in this analysis

Distractors were engineered to share vocabulary with facts, which likely makes
absolute hit rates pessimistic. Queries are single-shot paraphrases with one
correct answer; real agents ask multi-part questions. Embedding model
(MiniLM) is held constant; a stronger model could shift item 1's size.
